# AI the vL Way brand and voice standard (selected rules)

The published brand and voice standard for AI the vL Way. This is what this cartridge audits a piece of marketing copy against. Each rule below is quoted verbatim from the canonical brand documents, wrapped in an anchor so a finding can cite it.

## Verbatim rules

<!-- verbatim:brand-palette -->
the secondary colour changed from Deep Teal to Deep Navy (#14314F). Navy + Cognac is the pairing going forward.
<!-- /verbatim -->

**Accuracy note on brand-palette:** the Brand Package's own end-of-document "Quick Reference" line is stale. It still lists "Cognac (#C67A3C) · Deep Teal (#1D7A6E) · Warm Charcoal (#2C2926) · Warm White (#F7F3EE)," which predates a 2026-08-03 update recorded earlier in the same document. That update (quoted above, verbatim, dated) is the real, current statement: Deep Teal was replaced by Deep Navy (#14314F), paired with Cognac. Reading the update together with the Primary Colours table gives the current three core colours: Cognac (#C67A3C), Deep Navy (#14314F), Warm White (#F7F3EE). That three-colour summary is accurate, but it is my own synthesis of the table and the update note, not a single continuous sentence anywhere in the source. Only the sentence inside the anchor above is the actual verbatim quote this cartridge cites.

<!-- verbatim:brand-no-tech-blue -->
What to avoid: Gradients or 3D effects; neon, glow, or anything that screams "AI"; overly decorative fonts; circuit board patterns, binary code, or robot imagery; neon or electric "tech" blue (the cliché every AI brand uses).
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-typography -->
Headings in DM Sans Bold, Warm Charcoal or Cognac, sentence case always.
<!-- /verbatim -->

**Accuracy note on brand-typography:** this is the literal, current text of the Brand Package's "Type Rules" line, and it still says Warm Charcoal. A separate, later, dated note elsewhere in the same document (2026-08-04) states plainly that "dark text and headings now use Deep Navy (#14314F), not Warm Charcoal" and that "Warm Charcoal is retired from text/heading use." The Type Rules line itself was never edited to match that later change; the source document is internally inconsistent on this one point. This cartridge quotes the Type Rules sentence exactly as written rather than silently substituting Navy for Charcoal into the quote, because editing a quote to match what's "probably now correct" is exactly the kind of drift this whole method exists to prevent. Treat "Warm Charcoal" in the anchor above as superseded by the 2026-08-04 update for heading colour specifically; the font (DM Sans Bold) and case (sentence case) parts of the same sentence are not affected by that update and stay accurate as quoted.

<!-- verbatim:brand-thought-partner -->
Frame AI as a "thought partner," never a "tool" or "solution."
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-anti-patterns -->
Anti-patterns (never do): Hype, fear-based messaging, guru posturing, corporate buzzwords, emojis, blue/purple tech-bro aesthetics, stock robot images, long preambles, filler praise
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-canadian-english -->
Canadian English spelling and conventions.
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-voice-emdash -->
Never use em dashes. They are one of the clearest tells of AI writing. Use periods, commas, or parentheses instead.
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-voice-honestly -->
Never front-load "honestly," "to be honest," or "truthfully." They add nothing and are a dead giveaway of AI writing.
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-voice-buzzwords -->
Never use corporate buzzwords: no "leverage," "synergize," "circle back," "move the needle," "align on," "net-net," "deep dive" (unless used ironically).
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-voice-filler-praise -->
Never use filler praise: no "Great question!" or "That's a fantastic point!"
<!-- /verbatim -->

<!-- verbatim:brand-voice-emoji -->
Never use emojis in professional or working communication.
<!-- /verbatim -->

## What this cartridge can and cannot judge

This cartridge audits written marketing COPY (a caption, a landing blurb, an ad) against the brand and voice rules above. It does not judge the factual or legal accuracy of a claim in the copy, and it does not judge a rendered visual (it reads text, not pixels). A claim whose truth it cannot check from the text is flagged OUT_OF_SCOPE, not passed and not failed.
