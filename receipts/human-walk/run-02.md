# Human walk, run 02: a non-expert verifies the tool, then out-audits it

The raw, unedited transcript this write-up is drawn from is in `run-02-transcript.md`, so a reader can check the quotes below against the source the same way they check any finding against `reference/`.

Round 01 (in this folder) was the builder's own walkthrough. This run is the one that matters: a real non-technical person who had never seen the folder, given only the card and the law file, on a recorded transcript, with no coaching.

A note on who the tester is and how this was recorded, disclosed up front because a careful reader will check the metadata. Michelle is my partner, and the Granola meeting is filed under my account because it was the simplest device to record on. I would rather state that plainly than have it discovered. She is not a stranger, and I am not going to claim she is. What makes this a real blind test is not distance from me, it is that she had zero prior exposure and no hand in the result: she took no part in creating this folder, had never seen it, the tool, or the law before the walk, and was not coached (I did not speak during the walk). She is non-technical. The strongest evidence that this is a genuine blind test and not a friendly audience is in the transcript itself: she opens by disagreeing with the tool ("I feel like you should be allowed to say you can't bring kids"), and only changes her mind after reading the law herself. A friendly plant does not start by arguing with the thing it is there to endorse.

## Round 1: does the AI's finding match the law?
She was shown one finding ("No children please..." flagged FAIL, familial status, 100.75c1) and asked to confirm it against the law herself.

She started by DISAGREEING with the tool, out loud:
> "I feel like you should be allowed to say you can't bring kids."

Then she opened the law file and read the provision herself, and reasoned her own way to the answer:
> "Familial status. Well I guess if familial status means you have kids then this is a broken rule. Yes I agree."

- Can she find the provision? Yes, opened the law file and located 24 CFR 100.75 without being told where.
- Can she compare it herself? Yes, read the rule's text and mapped "no children" to familial status on her own.
- Does she reach the right verdict? Yes: "yes it does" (the finding matches).
- Does she trust it, and why? "Yeah I will trust it."

The important part is the order. She did not take the tool's word for it. She doubted it, opened the real law, and the law itself changed her mind. That is the whole design working on a real person: the trust comes from what she could verify, not from the AI asserting it.

## Round 2: she becomes the auditor
She was given a different line and the full law file, and asked to reach her own verdict before the tool's answer was revealed.

Line: "Ideal Christian home, perfect for a faith-filled family."

She reacted, opened the law, searched it, and committed to a verdict on her own, before the reveal:
> "No I don't think you can do that... let's go back and make my verdict. I have said that you are not allowed to advertise like that because you cannot talk about religious status."

That is FAIL, religion, reasoned independently from the law. Then the tool's hidden answer was revealed (FAIL, religion, 100.75c1). Her reaction:
> "Yes I agree with AI."

And her closing, unprompted:
> "The AI is very trustworthy and doing a good job."

## Why this is the receipt that counts
Two different things are proven on one recording, by one untrained non-expert with no prior exposure to the folder:
1. She can VERIFY the tool: given a finding, she confirmed it against the real law by eye.
2. She can MATCH the tool: given a fresh line and the law, she reached the same verdict the AI did, on her own, before seeing the AI's answer.

No expertise, no coaching, and she began skeptical. If a non-expert can both check the tool and independently arrive at the same call from the same standard, then the standard is doing the work and a person can stand behind the result. That is what a compliance tool has to prove, and it is proven here on tape.
